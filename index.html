What am I looking at?

Galileo is Europe's GNSS: about 30 satellites in medium Earth orbit (~23,222 km altitude, 56° inclination, 3 orbital planes). Each orbit takes ~14 h 05 min, so from Toulouse a Galileo satellite stays in view for hours, and 6–10 of them are usually above the mask.

Starlink satellites fly in low Earth orbit (~480–560 km), circling the Earth in ~95 min. A single pass over Toulouse lasts only minutes, so coverage needs thousands of them. Only a sample is drawn here; the count is scaled up to estimate the full fleet.

ECEF (Earth-Centred, Earth-Fixed) rotates with the Earth. It's the frame GNSS receivers output and the one your position is expressed in. Orbits drawn in it twist into loops because the ground turns underneath.

ICRF (International Celestial Reference Frame) is fixed with respect to distant quasars. Here the orbits are clean ellipses, the stars stand still, and the Earth spins inside them. Orbit dynamics live in this frame.

Implementation: SGP4 (satellite.js) outputs TEME coordinates. These are rotated to ECEF using GMST. The inertial view uses TEME-of-date as a stand-in for ICRF; the two differ by precession/nutation (≈0.4° in 2026), which is invisible at this scale. Elevation mask: 10°. Observer: Toulouse Capitole, 43.6045° N, 1.4440° E.
